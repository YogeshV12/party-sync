Frequently asked questions

How do I start?

On the phone with the mic, tap Be the mic and show the QR code. On every other phone, tap Join as a speaker and scan it. The phones join the host's Wi-Fi, sync their clocks and start playing together.

Do I need an internet connection?

No. Everything runs over a local Wi-Fi network or the host's hotspot. All phones must be on the same network.

Can I use Bluetooth speakers?

Yes. Connect the speaker to a phone the way you normally would, and that phone will play through it. Bluetooth adds delay, so use the speaker lag correction to line it up with the other phones.

The audio is out of sync or echoing between phones.

Use the speaker lag correction and sync delay controls in the app to fine-tune the room. Bluetooth speakers usually need the most correction. Placing phones very close together makes small delays easier to hear.

A phone can't connect or the QR code won't scan.
  • Check that the phone is on the same Wi-Fi network or hotspot as the host.
  • Allow Camera and Local Network access when asked. On iPhone, you can change these in Settings > Crowd Speaker.
  • Hold the phone steady about 20–30 cm from the QR code with good lighting.
  • Some public or guest Wi-Fi networks block devices from talking to each other. Try the host's hotspot instead.
How many people can join?

It depends on your Wi-Fi network. The host's live view shows who is connected and how healthy each connection is. Android phones can also relay the signal to reach more people.

Does it keep working with the screen off?

On Android, a notification keeps the session running in the background. For the most reliable audio, keep the app open during a session.

Do you record my voice or collect my data?

No. There are no accounts, analytics or ads, and your voice is never recorded or sent to any server. See the Privacy Policy.

How do I delete my data?

The app only stores a few settings on your own phone. Uninstalling the app removes them. We hold no data about you.

Which devices are supported?

iPhones and Android phones. iPhones can host and listen; Android phones can also relay the signal to reach more people.
